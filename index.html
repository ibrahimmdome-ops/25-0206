The Art of Typography

Chapter One — Origins

Typography is the craft of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and visually appealing. Its roots stretch back to the invention of moveable type by Johannes Gutenberg in the mid-fifteenth century, a moment that forever changed how knowledge was recorded and shared across the world.

The Gutenberg Press

Before the printing press, manuscripts were copied by hand in scriptoria — rooms within monasteries where monks laboured for years to reproduce a single volume. The press mechanised this process, reducing the time and cost of book production by orders of magnitude and spreading literacy throughout Europe.

✦ Key insight: Gutenberg's Bible, completed around 1455, is considered one of the most beautiful examples of early typography. Its two-column layout and careful spacing set a standard that modern designers still reference today.

Chapter Two — The Digital Age

From Metal to Pixels

The transition from physical type-setting to digital typography began in the 1980s with the rise of desktop publishing. Software like PageMaker and the PostScript language gave designers unprecedented control over letterforms, spacing, and layout — all on a personal computer screen.
